---

Before you send a resume, open **Check** in the editor. It shows how reliably an applicant tracking system (ATS) can read your resume, pins each problem to the line it's about, and compares your resume with a job posting. Everything except the optional writing review runs in your browser.

## Open Check

In the editor bar, select **Check**, or press <kbd>3</kbd> when you aren't typing in a field. The tab shows how many issues are open, or a check mark when there are none.

Check updates as you edit. Switch to **Write**, change something, and the score and issues follow straight away.

## Read the score

The panel starts with a score from 0 to 100 and a short verdict: **Reads cleanly** (80 and above), **Mostly readable** (50 to 79) or **Hard for software to read** (below 50). While any entry **Looks like part of the entry above**, **Reads cleanly** is blocked: scores of 50 or more read **Mostly readable**, while scores below 50 still read **Hard for software to read**.

<Frame caption="Check shows issues beside the affected resume">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/checking-your-resume/score-and-issues.webp"
    alt="Check mode with readability score 82, four issues, suggested fixes and numbered markers on the fictional sample resume"
  />
</Frame>

The score is the share of checks your resume passes, for example **19 of 23 checks pass**. There are 23 checks, grouped into five categories:

| Category             | What it looks at                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Contact details**  | Your name, email, phone and location are there, email and links are in a form software recognises, and whether a photo is showing.                                        |
| **Dates**            | Every experience and education entry has dates, in a form software reads, that don't run backwards or start in the future.                                                |
| **Layout**           | Every section with entries is placed on a page, text reads in order (sidebars, multi-column sections), and body text size, line height and page margins aren't too small. |
| **Section headings** | Headings are ones software looks for, such as Experience, Education and Skills, and some work history shows.                                                              |
| **Writing**          | Every role describes what you did, and no entry is a sentence an import split off the entry above it.                                                                     |

The heading check only runs when your resume's language is English, so resumes in other languages have 22 checks.

<Note>
  The score measures how reliably software can read your resume. It doesn't predict whether you'll be shortlisted.
</Note>

Below the issues, **Checks by category** lists each category with how many checks pass, or how many need a look. Select a row to read what it covers.

## Fix issues

The **Issues** tab lists every open issue as a numbered card, most serious first. Each card names its category, says what's wrong in plain words, and offers a fix.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the issue on the page">
    Each issue about a specific part of your resume has a matching numbered pin in the page margin, with a wavy underline under that part. Select a card, or its **Show on page** button, to outline the line on the page and scroll to it. Select a pin on the page to jump to its card.

    <Frame caption="Jump from an issue to its place on the page">
      <img src="/images/guides/checking-your-resume/issue-pinned-on-page.webp" alt="Check issue selected and outlined at its location on the fictional sample page" />
    </Frame>

  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply the fix">
    Many issues fix in one step. The card's main button says what it does, for example **Hide the photo**, **Use one column**, **Switch to one column** or **Use the standard heading**. A message confirms the change and offers **Undo**.

    Other issues need you to write something, such as a missing phone number or a role with no description. Their button, for example **Add a phone number** or **Describe the role**, switches to **Write** and opens the right field.

    After a PDF import, a bullet can arrive as an entry of its own: no dates, and a company or title cut out of a sentence. It ends on a comma or a word such as "by", or starts with a word such as "and". It can also run on for more than 10 words right after a line cut on a comma, a dash or a word such as "by" or "through", or read as prose, opening on an ordinary lower-case word, right after a line left without a full stop. Bullets written without full stops throughout don't count as left open, and a name that starts lower-case, such as **eBay** or **iOS**, isn't prose. Check reports it as **Looks like part of the entry above** instead of asking for dates, because dates would make it look like a separate job. A long or lower-case heading on its own, such as **eBay** or **Senior Vice President of Global Engineering Operations**, is a real entry, and Check asks for its dates. An entry with dates, a link or roles of its own is never reported this way, so merging never loses them. **Merge into previous entry** adds its text to the end of the entry above it and removes it; **Undo** in the message puts it back until you make another edit, and the builder's **Undo** after that. If it really is its own entry, select **Ignore** and add its dates in **Write**.

    <Frame caption="A sentence an import split off the entry above">
      <img src="/images/guides/checking-your-resume/fragment-entry.webp" alt="Issue card in the Writing category titled Looks like part of the entry above, quoting an entry that ends mid-sentence with processing time by, explaining it has no dates and reads like a sentence cut off the entry above, with Merge into previous entry, Show on page and Ignore" />
    </Frame>

  </Step>
  <Step title="Or set the issue aside">
    If an issue doesn't apply to you, select **Ignore**. For a choice that is yours to make, such as a two-column design, the button reads **Keep** instead. Ignored issues no longer count against the score. To bring them back, select **Show them again** under the list.
  </Step>
</Steps>

When every check passes, the tab reads **Nothing to fix.**

<Tip>
  On a phone, selecting a pin opens a bar at the top of the page that reads "Issue 1 of 3". Use the arrows to step
  through the issues and fix each one from the card at the bottom, without leaving the page.
</Tip>

## See what a parser reads

At the top of the page, switch from **What a person sees** to **What a parser reads**. The page is replaced by the text software pulls out of your resume's PDF, in the order it comes out, with the name, email, phone, location, links, section headings and dates it recognised.

<Frame caption="Review the text a parser reads">

Use it to spot content that lands in the wrong place, for example a sidebar read after your experience, or a field picked up from the wrong line. Switch back with **What a person sees**.

## Match your resume to a job

The **Job match** tab picks out the terms a job posting stresses and shows which ones your resume already has. It isn't part of the score.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Give it a posting">
    If the resume is linked to an application that has a saved posting, Job match uses that posting. If the linked application has no posting yet, paste it into the box and select **Save to the application**. Otherwise, choose an application from the list, or paste the posting into the box and select **Match this posting**.

    A pasted posting is kept for this visit only. To keep it, select **Save as a job…** and enter the **Company** and **Role**. **Save job and make a copy** saves the job and a copy of this resume linked to it, together (if either can't be saved, neither is), and opens the copy on Job match, so you tailor the copy and this resume stays as it is for other jobs. Trying again after a dropped connection doesn't save the job or the copy twice. **Save and link this resume** links this resume itself instead.

    Choosing an application for a resume that isn't that job's copy asks the same thing: **Make a copy for this job** or **Link this resume**. Unsaved edits are saved before the copy is made.

    <Frame caption="Saving a pasted posting as a job, with a copy to tailor">
      <img src="/images/guides/checking-your-resume/job-match-save-as-job.webp" alt="Job match with a pasted posting, Company Northwind Labs and Role filled in, Save job and make a copy, Save and link this resume and Cancel, above 5 of 19 posting terms appear and the missing and covered terms" />
    </Frame>

  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the terms">
    The tab shows how many posting terms appear, for example **19 of 25 posting terms appear**. Terms under **Not in your resume · add only if true** are missing. Terms under **Already covered** are there already: select one to highlight where it appears on the page.

    The count leaves out what a resume shouldn't claim: the employer's name, the job's title and the job's location (from the linked application, or from a pasted posting: "Fieldnote is hiring", "Engineer at Fieldnote", or a title's tail after a dash that the posting also names as the employer or that ends like a company name, such as "Acme Corp"; a tail such as "Staff Engineer — Observability" is the field and still counts), plain numbers such as "12+", lines about pay and perks such as salary, equity, holiday and relocation, and single generic words such as "drive", "grow", "evolve", "analyze" or "background". A hyphenated phrase in your resume counts both ways: "checkout-flow" covers "checkout flows". A skill that contains one counts whole, such as "big data", "hybrid cloud" or "technical lead", and so does a pair the posting repeats, such as "help desk" or "early childhood". A tool named after one of those words still counts when the posting writes it as a name: "Experience managing Workday" counts Workday. Everyday words that are also tool names, such as "rails" or "next", count only when the posting writes them as a name, such as "Go, Java" or a bullet starting "Go and Kubernetes". This is a word comparison, not a judgement of fit.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a missing term, if it's true">
    Select a missing term to see how often the posting uses it, then choose:

    - **Add to Skills** to add it as a keyword on your first visible skill, which the button names (for example **Add to Skills · Unity Engine**). If you have no skills, it creates one. It's offered only for known skill names; a phrase from the posting, such as "accessibility audits", belongs in an entry instead.
    - **Ask the assistant to work it in** to open the Assistant, which asks you before it adds anything. A pasted posting is sent with the request, since the assistant can't otherwise see it.
    - **Not true for me, hide it** to leave it out of the match. Hidden terms are listed under the tab with **Show again**.

  </Step>
</Steps>

  <img

    alt="Job match tab ready to compare resume text with a job posting"
  />
</Frame>

Once a resume is linked, the top of the tab names the application. Select **Change** to link a different application or to unlink it.

## Check the exported PDF

The checks above read your resume's content and settings. To test the actual file a recruiter receives, select **Also check the exported PDF** at the bottom of the **Issues** tab. Reactive Resume creates the PDF in your browser and runs the full file check on it, the same one the [ATS checker](/guides/using-the-ats-checker) uses. Nothing is uploaded.

A message tells you whether the PDF reads cleanly or how many more things there are to look at. Anything it finds is pinned to the page with a PDF icon and a dashed outline.

<Frame caption="The file check reports an item; Show opens its report">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/checking-your-resume/exported-pdf-pin.webp"
    alt="Check after generating the actual PDF, with Exported PDF checked: 1 more to look at and Show"
  />
</Frame>

Select **Show** in the message, or any PDF pin, to open the full report. It gives a score out of 100, a score for each category (**Readability**, **Layout**, **Sections**, **Contact details**, **Dates**), and each finding with what to do about it. **Writing** holds tips that don't affect the score.

<Frame caption="Review the actual PDF report and each finding">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/checking-your-resume/exported-pdf-report.webp"
    alt="The exported PDF report with score 100, category scores and a date-format warning for the fictional resume"
  />
</Frame>

The pins show while the **Issues** tab is open, until you change the resume. After an edit, run the check again.

## Get a second opinion on your wording

The checks are mechanical. The **Writing** tab asks an AI model how a reader might react to your summary and bullets, and suggests rewrites. It needs your own AI provider; see [Connecting an AI provider](/guides/using-ai). Without one, the tab offers **Open AI settings**. Issues and Job match work without AI.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the model">
    The tab says where your resume's text goes, for example "Sends your resume's text only to OpenAI · gpt-5-mini, with your key." To use another of your providers, select **Change** and pick it.

    <Frame caption="Writing review is separate from the readability score">
      <img src="/images/guides/checking-your-resume/writing-review-start.webp" alt="Writing review tab explaining the optional AI review and provider connection" />
    </Frame>

  </Step>
  <Step title="Run the review">
    Select **Review writing**. The review reads up to 120 bullets and paragraphs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Accept or reject the suggestions">
    Rewrites of your own bullets appear as **Proposed edits**, numbered in the page margin. Select **Accept** or **Reject** on each, or **Accept all** or **Reject all**. **Undo** next to a rejected edit makes it undecided again. With an edit focused, <kbd>A</kbd> accepts it, <kbd>R</kbd> rejects it, and <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> move between edits.

    Advice that isn't a rewrite appears as a note marked **High**, **Medium** or **Low**, with **Show on page**. **What's working** lists your strengths. Select **Run again** for a fresh review.

  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  A model's opinion can be wrong, and the review never changes the score. Accept only the rewrites that are true to your
  experience.
</Warning>

## Reopen a Writing review

The latest Writing review is saved with its input and review time. It remains readable after reload or when its provider is off. If the resume has changed, the review is marked out of date; original passages and suggestions remain visible. Review again before applying an out-of-date suggestion. Issues and Job match remain available without AI.

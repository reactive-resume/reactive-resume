---
title: "Using the assistant"
description: "Ask the AI assistant to improve your resume or cover letter, then review and accept or reject its proposed changes. Nothing changes until you accept."
---

The assistant is an AI chat panel beside your document in the editor. It reads the resume or letter you have open, and the job posting when the document is linked to an application, then proposes changes for you to review. Text-only suggestions can be accepted individually; changes to fields, entries or sections are reviewed and accepted as one set. It never changes your document on its own.

## Before you start

Connect an AI provider with your own API key. See [Connecting an AI provider](/guides/using-ai). If you open the assistant before connecting one, it offers to connect one on the spot.

## Open the assistant

In the editor, select **Assistant** (the sparkle button) in the editor bar, just before **Share**, or press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd> on Windows and Linux). Press it again, or select **Close the assistant** (×) in the panel, to close it.

On wide screens the assistant opens as a third column. On smaller laptops it takes the place of the left panel, on tablets it slides in as a drawer, and on phones it fills the screen.

You can also reach it from elsewhere:

- In the command bar (<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd>), type a question and choose **Ask the assistant**. Outside the editor, this opens the document you edited last and sends the question there. See [Using the command bar](/guides/using-the-command-bar).

For job search and interview coaching, open **Career → Coach**, or select **Open workspace** in an application and then **Ask the coach**. That coach works with your Knowledge and application context. This guide covers editing resumes and letters; [Career and Application Workspaces](/guides/career-overview) covers the career coach.

## Ask for a change

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick a suggestion or write your own">
    A new conversation starts with a few suggestions, such as **Find weak bullets**, **Tighten to one page** and **Draft a summary** for a resume, or **Make the opening stronger** and **Make it shorter** for a letter. When the document is linked to an application, **Tailor to the … posting** appears first.

    Or type in the box (**Ask, or describe a change…**) and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Use <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd> for a new line.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Let it read and reply">
    The assistant reads your document, then answers in a sentence or two and shows its suggestions as a set of proposed edits. You can keep editing your document while it works.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Specific requests work best: "Rewrite my summary for a senior frontend role", "Make the bullets in my latest job lead with results", or "Shorten this letter to under 250 words". When you only ask a question, such as "Which bullet is the weakest?", the assistant answers without proposing edits.

For resumes, you can also ask to add entries or custom sections, or change your name, headline, section titles and entry text fields. For example, "Add a Patents section with this patent" creates a custom section with structured entries; it does not put a heading inside a project description. Languages, Certifications and Skills use their own typed entries. Create or open cover letters through **Documents**; they cannot be added as resume sections. Hidden summary, sections and entries are excluded from the assistant's document view. Show them in Write first if you want to work on them.

Requests involving fields, entries or sections appear as one set showing the affected fields. Review the set, then accept or reject it together. If the resume's text or structure changes before accepting, ask again for a fresh set; this avoids overwriting your newer changes. Text-only resume suggestions and cover-letter suggestions still work paragraph by paragraph.

Field suggestions identify the entry and role they change. Entry suggestions show the stored fields, including proficiency levels, icons and colors, and each set keeps the reasons for its changes. A text edit adds or rewrites one paragraph or bullet, supplied on one line. Ambiguous multiline records and section headings inside that text are declined; ask for a new section instead. Quoted text such as `"## Languages"` remains ordinary text.

Some fields, including existing dates, website fields, keyword lists and page language, still need **Write** or **Design**. Select **Open Write** in the assistant: **Basics** holds name, headline and contact details; each section holds its entries; **Add section** adds a missing section. You can also import an old resume from Documents to fill a blank resume.

The assistant cannot translate a whole resume. It can translate summary, descriptions and supported text fields; keywords, nested fields, generated date labels and page language require separate changes in Write or Design. Accepting translated text does not finish a whole-resume translation.

## Review proposed edits

A text-only suggestion shows where it lands (for example, **Summary · paragraph 1**), the current text struck through, the new text highlighted, and a short reason. These text changes are also marked on the page, with a **proposed** badge in their section.

A request involving fields, entries or sections appears as one card for the entire supported set. The card lists the affected entries and roles, the current field values, the proposed values and the reasons. New entries show their stored fields, including date facts and link destinations. Review this card before accepting: the page previews the proposed resume, but structural changes do not have the individual old/new markings or section badges used for text-only suggestions.

- Select **Accept** to apply a text edit or an entire structural set, or **Reject** to dismiss it.
- For text-only suggestions with more than one edit waiting, **Accept all** applies the edits it can still place. The notification counts those edits and tells you how many could not be placed. A structural set has one Accept control for all its changes.
- With an edit focused, press <kbd>A</kbd> to accept, <kbd>R</kbd> to reject, and <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> to move between edits.

Accepted edits show **Applied** and the set's title changes to, for example, **1 of 1 applied**. They stay applied when you refine the wording yourself. If you change a passage yourself before deciding, its edit shows **Out of date: the text has changed since.** A pending structural set also becomes out of date when resume content or layout changes. Ask again to get a fresh suggestion.

The assistant only rewrites what your document already says. When a posting asks for something your document doesn't mention, it asks you first instead of inventing it.

Select **Edit** to change a text edit's new text in your own words before deciding, then **Save wording**. A structural set has no **Edit**: accept it and adjust the result in Write, or reject it and ask again. Once it's saved and the app has read it back, the card shows **Edited by you**, and **Accept** applies your wording. If saving or reading it back fails, your text stays in the box with **Try again**, which picks up wording that was already saved. While its wording is open, saving or unsaved, the edit can't be accepted by any route, including **Accept all** and the <kbd>A</kbd> key, until it's saved or you cancel. An accepted edit is recorded with the document save itself, and only when it was applied in that edit's current wording. It stays recorded when other edits land in the same place, when you refine it, and through Undo and Redo. The same holds for a structural set you refine after accepting. If another tab or device changed the wording first, the save is refused and nothing is written: the editor shows a version conflict, where you can keep your draft as a copy and reload. Undo works the same way as for any accepted edit.

### Tailoring to a posting

Tailoring changes a copy of your resume made for that job, never the resume your other applications use. If the open resume isn't that copy, the assistant shows **Copy for this job** above the message box and won't propose edits that use the posting until you copy it, or leave the posting out. Edits it proposed with a posting can't be accepted on a resume that isn't that job's copy: copy the resume for the job and ask again there. Edits from older conversations that may have used a posting show as out of date and can't be applied anywhere; ask again on the resume you want to change.

<Note>
  An older version of the app can't check these edits. A resume or letter with tailored edits it can't accept, or with
  wording you changed, refuses changes from an older version of the app, even ones typed by hand: that app shows a
  version conflict and keeps its draft as a copy. Refresh the page, or update the mobile app, to keep editing that
  document. Changes an older version kept on your device while offline are still treated as that version's after you
  update: such a document refuses them the same way. On mobile, **This older draft needs review** offers **Keep both**
  to save them as a separate document, or **Use saved version** to discard them and start from the server's copy. **Keep
  mine** isn't available for that refusal. On the web, **Review saved version** leads to the existing recovery choices.
  Documents without such edits work as before.
</Note>

When you tailor, the card can also show:

- **The assistant's reading of n posting requirements:** the requirements it found in the posting (and any the application lists), marked **An edit addresses it**, **Cited as already shown · check it**, **No evidence found · left as a gap** or **Not assessed**. Gaps stay open, even when no edit could be placed; the assistant doesn't fill them with experience you don't have. It can miss requirements in a posting's text, so read the posting too.
- **Posting asks for:** the requirement an edit addresses.
- **The assistant cites n sources · check they say this:** the passages, or Knowledge facts, the assistant pointed to. A citation is the assistant's pointer, not proof; read it before you accept. **Nothing cited for this edit** means it pointed to nothing.
- **Nothing checked backs these. Confirm each before accepting:** what the model flagged, plus numbers and years unless one statement in the entry (or role) being edited says all of them with the same subject words (longer words, and short names such as AWS), and words that aren't anywhere in your resume. A reworded claim can be flagged too. These checks find likely problems; they can't confirm an edit is true.

Accepting changes the document only. It never saves anything to Knowledge as a confirmed fact. This reading helps you edit; it's separate from the term list in Check, and it isn't a prediction of how an employer or screening system will respond.

### Use Knowledge

Turn on **Use Knowledge** above the message box to let the assistant look up your switched-on [Knowledge](/guides/building-career-knowledge) facts and cite them. It's off for each new conversation. When it's on, facts the assistant looks up are sent to your AI provider with that message. Once a reply looks up Knowledge, that reply and every later one in the conversation are treated as using it, because they can repeat it. They aren't sent again with a message that leaves Knowledge off. If you later correct, switch off or forget a fact, those replies are hidden from the conversation and never sent again, and a reply waiting on your answer has to start over.

## Undo an accepted edit

Accepting shows a notification with the number of edits applied and **Undo**. You can also press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd>) like any other change, or use History to restore an earlier document. An edit you explicitly undo becomes pending again when its original passage is available, so you can accept it later. That decision is saved with the document, even if you close the assistant. If an unsaved decision is recovered after another tab or device saved a newer version, saving pauses for the usual conflict controls; matching text alone does not choose which decision to keep. See [Undoing changes and version history](/guides/undoing-changes-and-version-history).

## Answer a question

When the assistant needs a fact or a choice from you, it shows a blue question card with a few answers. Select one, or type your own under **Or in your own words…** and select **Send**. The assistant then continues with your answer.

The same question cards appear in **Career → Coach** and **Ask the coach** inside an application workspace, on web and mobile. Only the latest reply can take an answer. Choices stay disabled while a reply is starting or reconnecting; **Stop** cancels that wait.

If a question did not finish or could not be asked, send a new message to continue. If the provider fails after you answer, **Try again** keeps that answer and retries the reply. After reopening a conversation whose answer was already saved, send a new message instead.

## Choose what's shared with each message

Above the message box, chips show what goes with your next message: the document, and the job posting when the document is linked to an application. Select the **×** on a chip to leave it out, including before your first message. Without the document, the assistant can't read or edit it for that message. The line under the box always says what will be sent and to which provider, and nothing is sent until you press send.

Removing either context chip also leaves earlier conversation history out of the next reply. Pending questions or approvals from a different context cannot resume; send a new message with your current choices.

When you select **Prepare** from an application's detail panel, the assistant uses that application's posting, even when several applications share the same resume.

## Attach files

Select the paperclip in the message box to attach files, such as a job description PDF or your notes. You can attach them before your first message or later in the conversation. Each file shows as a chip; select its **×** to remove it before sending.

- Plain text, Markdown and JSON files are read as text (up to the first 40,000 characters).
- Images, PDFs and MP3 or WAV audio are passed to the model directly. Whether the model can use them depends on your provider.
- Other file types are attached but not read. Paste the relevant text instead.
- You can attach up to 10 files per message, each up to 25 MB, 100 MB per conversation and 1 GB across all your conversations.

<Note>
  Attachments work with local, S3-compatible or Vercel Blob storage and stay private. If you use a reverse proxy, allow
  request bodies of at least 50 MB: files travel base64-encoded, which makes them about a third larger.
</Note>

## Search the web

If you connect an optional **Web access** service in Integrations, the assistant uses that selected service for search and enhanced reading. If your installation's administrator set up server search, the assistant searches with it instead, with or without a connection. **Web access** then says **Your server provides search for Applications and the assistant**, and, until you connect a service, **Test search** checks it. Without a connection, the assistant can read supplied public URLs with the built-in reader or work from text you paste. See [Assistant tools](/guides/ai-agent-tools#web-search-support).

You can also supply a public page URL: the assistant uses the selected reader or built-in reading. Page reading does not require a separate search account. Failed reading leads to pasted-text recovery; clipped or incomplete pages are identified before you rely on them.

Before each search, a card shows the exact query. Select **Search the web** to send that query to your search service, or **Skip** to continue without it. Review it for private details before approving. This applies on web and mobile.

The conversation shows search/read progress, failures and retrieved **Sources**. Sources remain visible after reopening the conversation. A failed search keeps your conversation and does not silently switch paid providers.

## Switch models

The model chip in the assistant's header shows the model in use. Select it to switch to another connected provider for this conversation, or choose **Connect another…** to add one in settings.

## Stop, retry or copy a conversation

- **Stop a reply** with the stop button that replaces send while the assistant is working, or press <kbd>Esc</kbd> in the message box. If it stopped before proposing anything, you'll see **Stopped. No edits were proposed.** with **Continue**.
- **When the provider returns an error**, your message is kept. Select **Retry**, or **Switch model** to try another provider.
- **Copy transcript** at the end of the conversation copies the messages as plain text, speaker by speaker, without the tool steps, and includes the retrieved source links.

A single reply can run for about four minutes. If it hits that limit, the assistant says "Time limit reached. Your progress is saved. Ask me to continue."

## Past conversations

Each conversation belongs to one document. Opening the assistant continues that document's latest conversation. Select **New conversation** (the pencil) to start fresh, or **Past conversations** (the clock) to see earlier ones.

<Frame caption="Past conversations for this document">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/using-the-assistant/past-conversations.webp"
    alt="Past conversations list under This document, showing three conversations titled by their first message, each with its time and outcome such as 1 of 1 edits accepted or no edits"
  />
</Frame>

Conversations are titled by your first message and show how many edits you accepted. Conversations about your other documents appear under **Other documents**; opening one takes you to that document. To delete a conversation, hover over it, select **×**, then confirm. Its messages and attachments are deleted, but edits you accepted stay in your document.

A conversation becomes read-only if its document is locked or deleted, or its provider was deleted. Unlock the document, or pick another model from the model chip, to continue.

<Info>
  Replies stream while the conversation is open. On instances without Redis (some self-hosted setups), a reply that's
  still running when you reload the page can't be picked up again; its finished messages are still saved.
</Info>

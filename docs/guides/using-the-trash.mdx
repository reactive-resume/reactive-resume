---
title: "Using the Trash"
description: "Restore documents and applications within 30 days, or explicitly delete them for good."
---

When you remove a resume, cover letter or application, it goes to **Trash** instead of disappearing. It stays there for 30 days, so you can bring it back if you change your mind.

## Move a document to Trash

On Documents, open the document's **⋯** menu and choose **Move to Trash**. In the editor, open the document name menu at the top left and choose **Move to Trash**; you return to Documents.

To remove several documents at once, select their checkboxes in Documents, then choose **Move to Trash** in the selection bar. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> or select **×** to clear a selection without removing anything.

Reactive Resume doesn't ask you to confirm, because the move can be undone. A message appears at the bottom of the screen. Select **Undo** to put the document back straight away.

While a resume is in Trash, its public link stops working. Restoring it brings the resume back as it was.

<Note>Locked documents can't be moved to Trash. Choose **Unlock editing** in the document's menu first.</Note>

## Open the Trash

**Trash** always appears at the bottom of the sidebar. Select it to open the Trash page. Its count covers documents and applications; there is no count when it is empty. On a phone, the sidebar becomes a tab bar, so a **Trash** row with the count appears at the end of Documents instead, as in the Android and iPhone apps.

<Frame caption="Trash stays in the sidebar">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/using-the-trash/trash-in-sidebar.webp"
    alt="Calm sidebar with Search, Today, Documents, Applications, Career, Trash, Shortcuts and account settings"

You can also open the [command bar](/guides/using-the-command-bar) with <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> on Windows and Linux) and choose **Trash**, or go to `https://rxresu.me/dashboard/trash`. Self-hosters use their own address.

The Trash page has **All**, **Documents** and **Applications** filters. Each row shows its kind, when it moved to Trash and how many days are left. It loads up to 100 documents at a time; use **Previous** and **Next** below the rows to browse more. The sidebar count covers both kinds of item in Trash. When it's empty, it says **Trash is empty**.

Previous rows stay visible while another page loads, but selection and actions wait until the requested page arrives. If loading fails, choose **Try again**; the previous rows remain visible and unavailable for changes.

Select a row's checkbox to act on one document, or select several. **Select all** chooses the rows currently loaded on the page. The bar at the bottom offers **Restore** and **Delete permanently…** for that selection. **Empty Trash…** in the page header is separate: it deletes everything in Trash, not only the rows you selected.

## Restore a document

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select the documents">
    Tick the checkbox beside each document you want back, or select **Select all** for the loaded rows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose Restore">
    Select **Restore**. Restoring asks for no confirmation. The documents go back to Documents with their content, tags
    and links intact.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can still restore one document from its row menu. On the Trash page, select **⋯** at the end of the document's row, then choose **Restore**.

## Restore an application

Choose **Move to Trash** from an application's menu, or from its bulk selection controls. The move happens at once, without confirmation, and a message offers **Undo**. This saves the move on the server; reloading does not lose the recovery option.

Open **Trash** in the web sidebar and choose **Applications**, or open **Trash** on the native Applications screen. The Applications view loads 100 rows at a time, with **Previous** and **Next** for more.

Select **Restore** beside the application. Its identity, timeline, document links, submitted snapshots, workspace, application-specific Knowledge, coach history and attached bytes stay intact. The move itself changes no linked document. Later explicit edits or permanent deletion of a linked document can still change that relationship.

Applications stay in Trash for 30 days, then are deleted permanently. Applications already in Trash before this retention rule takes effect get 30 days from that date; their displayed time remaining can be longer during the transition. **Delete permanently…** asks for confirmation and removes the application and its dependent records. It keeps linked resumes, cover letters and their versions. **Empty Trash…** deletes both documents and applications, even with one kind filtered.

Restore and permanent deletion check the exact listed Trash generation. If another session restores or re-trashes the application, an older action fails instead of deleting or restoring the newer state. Refresh the list and review the current row before retrying. If the posting was tracked again while this application was in Trash, resolve that duplicate before restoring it.

Application schedules and notifications are hidden while it is in Trash; queued work is canceled and existing coach runs receive cancellation. Restore does not replay canceled work. Review upcoming schedules and turn device reminders back on where needed.

## Delete a document for good

Documents are deleted permanently 30 days after you moved them to Trash. To delete them sooner:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select what to delete">
    Tick the checkbox beside each document, or use **Select all** for the loaded rows. To delete everything in Trash instead, choose **Empty Trash…** in the page header.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Select **Delete permanently…** or **Empty Trash…**, then select the plain **Delete permanently** or **Empty Trash** button to confirm. Choose **Keep in Trash** to change your mind.

    <Frame caption="Permanent deletion asks with a plain button">
      <img src="/images/guides/using-the-trash/delete-now-confirmation.webp" alt="Delete Old draft permanently dialog with Keep in Trash and Delete permanently buttons" />
    </Frame>

  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  The confirmation names the selected rows for a selection, and says **Empty Trash?** with the total count for **Empty
  trash…**. The distinction matters when only some rows are loaded: Empty trash always covers the whole Trash.
</Note>

<Frame caption="Empty Trash covers documents and applications">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/using-the-trash/empty-trash-confirmation.webp"
    alt="Empty Trash confirmation naming three documents and one application, with Keep in Trash and Empty Trash buttons"
  />
</Frame>

<Warning>
  A deleted document can't be restored, and neither can its version history. If you might need it again, download a JSON
  copy first (see [Downloading your resume](/guides/exporting-your-resume)).
</Warning>

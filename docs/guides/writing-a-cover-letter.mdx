---
title: "Writing a cover letter"
description: "Create a cover letter as its own document, link it to a resume and a job application, match your resume's design, and download it."
---

In Reactive Resume, a cover letter is a document of its own. It sits next to your resumes in **Documents**, under the **Letters** tab. A letter can take your name, contact details and design from one of your resumes, and its recipient from a job application, so you only write the body.

## Create a letter

You can start a letter from an application or from **Documents**.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="From an application">
    Starting from the job you're applying for is quickest, because the letter is set up for that job.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the application">
        In **Applications**, select the application to open its details.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Select Write a letter">
        Open **Apply**, then select **Write a letter** if no letter is linked. The button shows only while the application has no letter. The **New document** dialog opens with this application already chosen.

        <Frame caption="Prepare documents from the application’s Apply screen">
          <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/application-write-a-letter.webp" alt="Fictional Kiln Studio application page with its preparation next step and seven Work on it rows" />
        </Frame>
      </Step>
      <Step title="Choose what to start from">
        Under **Start from**, choose an earlier letter’s body or keep **Blank letter**. Enter a **Name**, then select **Create letter**.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    The new letter uses the **Name** you entered. It becomes this application's letter, takes its recipient from the application (the company, and the first contact's name if there is one), and takes your details and design from the application's resume, if it has one. Rename the letter to show the company. When you start from an earlier letter, only its body is copied: your new letter keeps this job's company, name and date, and both letters keep separate History. The earlier letter is never changed.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="From Documents">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open New document">
        In **Documents**, select **New document**, or press <kbd>N</kbd> then <kbd>L</kbd>.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Choose Cover letter">
        Select **Cover letter**. Choose **For which job** and **Resume**, or keep **No job yet** and **No resume**.

        <Frame caption="Choose a job, resume and starting letter">
          <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/new-dialog-cover-letter.webp" alt="New document with Cover letter selected, job and resume choices, Blank letter, Name and Create letter" />
        </Frame>
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    Choose a **Resume**, or **No resume** to start independently. Under **Start from**, choose an earlier letter’s body or keep **Blank letter**. Enter a **Name**, then select **Create letter**. You can link an application and change the resume in the editor. Starting from an earlier letter copies only the body; both letters keep their own History, and the earlier letter is never changed. The Android and iPhone apps offer the same **Start from an earlier letter** choice when writing a letter for an application.

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

To bring back a letter you downloaded as JSON from Reactive Resume, import the file from **Import** on Documents as you would a resume; it comes back as a letter. See [Importing resumes](/guides/importing-resumes).

## Fill in the letter

The letter editor looks like the resume editor, with two modes: **Write** and **Design**. The panel on the left holds the letter's details, and the page on the right shows the letter as it will print, updating as you type.

<Frame caption="Write the letter beside its rendered page">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-editor-write.webp"
    alt="Kiln Studio letter for fictional Alex Morgan with Recipient, Sender, Letter and greeting controls beside the PDF"
  />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="For: the application">
    Under **For**, select **Link an application** to choose the job the letter is for, or **Change** to pick another. Closed applications aren't listed, except the one this letter is already on. Applications with a different letter already recorded as sent are disabled and labeled **Letter already sent**. A letter recorded as sent keeps its application link. Linking fills the recipient's company and name. Choose **No application** to unlink it. Without an application, you fill in the recipient yourself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="To: the recipient">
    Under **Recipient**, check **Name or team**, **Company** and **Date**. The greeting follows the name: "Dear Priya," for Priya Shah, or a greeting to the hiring team when the name is empty.
  </Step>
  <Step title="From: your details">
    Under **Sender**, choose the **Resume** your details come from. With **Use details from** that resume turned on, your name, headline, email, phone and location stay linked: when you change them on the resume, the letter changes too, and tells you the next time you open it.

    Turn the switch off to keep a copy of your details in the letter instead. Below the switch, edit everything the letter's header prints: **Full name**, **Headline**, **Email**, **Phone**, **Location**, **Website** and the extra contact fields and profile links under **Add field**. Clear a field, or remove an extra field, to leave it off the letter. If the resume has a photo, turn off **Show photo** to leave it off this letter; to change the photo itself, edit the resume. These edits change only this letter; your source resume stays unchanged. The copy doesn't change when the resume does.

    Choose **No resume** to write a standalone letter with the same sender fields. Removing a resume keeps the details and design currently shown. Sender edits update the preview, automatic signature and downloads, and save with the letter.

    Turning **Use details from** back on replaces the letter's independent details with the resume's current details. Choosing a resume for a standalone letter also replaces its sender details and links its design. Changing resumes while the sender switch is off keeps your independent details.

    <Frame caption="Sender and length stay with the letter">
      <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-from-resume.webp" alt="Letter panel showing details of the fictional Kiln Studio letter" />
    </Frame>

    <Frame caption="With No resume selected, sender fields belong to this letter. The preview and automatic signature use Jordan's saved details.">
      <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-sender-details.png" alt="Standalone letter with No resume selected, editable headline, website and profile links, fictional Jordan Reyes sender fields and the current Export action" />
    </Frame>

  </Step>
  <Step title="Letter: the body">
    Under **Letter**, select **Write it myself** and type the body. The greeting and sign-off are added around it in the letter's language. A letter created from an application starts in the posting's declared language, so the body, greeting, sign-off and date stay in one language. **Design → Language** changes the letter's language at any time, even while its design still mimics the resume: the design link shares template, type and colors, not the language, and your authored greeting, sign-off and words are never rewritten.

<Frame caption="Language is its own Design setting. This letter was created from a German posting, so its greeting, date and sign-off are German while its template, type and colors still match the resume.">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-design-language.png"
    alt="Letter Design panel with Match the resume on, a Language field set to German, and a German letter page with Einstellungsteam, 31. Oktober 2026 and Liebes Einstellungsteam"
  />
</Frame>

    Edit **Greeting** or **Sign-off** to use your own wording. **Sign-off** includes your name: edit the whole signature and add your phone, email or other details on lines below it. Custom signatures print exactly as entered, without an extra name appended. Clear either field to omit it, useful when you already wrote a closing in the body. Select **Use default** to restore the automatic wording and sender name.

    The body editor has the same formatting tools as the resume; see [Formatting text](/guides/formatting-text).

  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="Write the letter beside its rendered page">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-greeting-sign-off.png"
    alt="Kiln Studio letter for fictional Alex Morgan with Recipient, Sender, Letter and greeting controls beside the PDF"
  />
</Frame>

Below the body, **Length** counts your words against the 180 to 320 words most recruiters read, and says whether the letter is short, comfortable or getting long. It's a guide, not a rule.

<Frame caption="Write the letter beside its rendered page">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-length-and-design.webp"
    alt="Kiln Studio letter for fictional Alex Morgan with Recipient, Sender, Letter and greeting controls beside the PDF"
  />
</Frame>

Changes save automatically. The status under the letter's name in the editor bar shows **Saving…**, **Saved** or, if something went wrong, **Not saved** with **Retry**.

If saving stops with **Review saved version**, select **Reload**, then **Keep a copy and reload**. Your unsaved edits are saved as a separate letter in **Documents**, with “(mine)” added to its name, before the saved version opens. The copy keeps the text, sender details and design shown in your editor, without links to the resume or application. If copying or reloading fails, your edits stay open for retry. In the native app, **Keep both** offers the same copy-and-load choice.

## Draft the body with AI

If you've connected an AI provider, an empty body offers a first draft. See [Connecting an AI provider](/guides/using-ai) to set one up.

<Frame caption="Write the body yourself or choose optional AI drafting">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-body-empty.webp"
    alt="Empty letter draft with manual writing and optional drafting controls for the fictional linked application"
  />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start the draft">
    Select **Draft from the posting** when the letter is linked to an application, or **Draft from your resume** when it
    only has a resume. The draft uses only what the letter is linked to: the posting, your resume, or both, and it is
    written in the letter's language. The line beside the button names the provider it sends them to. Select **Stop** to
    end it early: what was written so far stays as a draft you can accept and finish yourself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Refine it">
    When the draft is ready, ask for **Shorter** or **More personal** versions, or select **Reject** to drop it. Earlier
    versions stay: use the arrows beside "2 of 3" to compare them, then accept or revise the one shown.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Accept it">
    Select **Accept** to put the draft in the body. Until you do, the draft never replaces anything, and you can edit it
    freely afterwards. An unkept draft survives a reload in the same tab: it comes back with **Accept**, **Shorter**,
    **More personal** and **Reject**, so seeing it again doesn't cost another request. Leaving the editor with a draft
    streaming or waiting asks you to keep or discard it first.
  </Step>
</Steps>

After accepting a body draft on an updated server, the body shows its recorded **AI request attempts**, original connection label, provider and requested model. Choosing an earlier version keeps that version's sources. A revision also keeps sources from the draft it revised. **Stop**, recovery, copying a letter and saving don't add provider requests. The weekly report still includes attempts for drafts you reject. Historical details stay after removing a connection.

An AI client using [the MCP application tools](/guides/managing-applications-with-mcp#use-ai-on-an-application) uses the same letter draft prompt. It receives text for you to review and an empty linked letter. The client must ask before keeping that body; after an approved save, its recorded request sources appear here too. Nothing is sent to the employer.

<Frame caption="The accepted body keeps its original request history even after the connection is removed.">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-ai-provenance.png"
    alt="Fictional services letter body showing one recorded AI request attempt from Fictional Letter AI using letter-model"
  />
</Frame>

Letter History keeps source references with each saved body. Restoring a version restores its references too; an older version without references has unknown history. Export everything includes these private references and request records. A letter's portable JSON and employer-facing downloads contain its document content, not private request history.

To draft, a letter needs a linked resume or application. After you accept a draft, **Draft from the posting** is gone
from the body; to start over, open the Assistant and choose **Redraft from the posting**, which proposes a new version
as edits to accept. In the body, **Improve** works on one paragraph and offers **Tie to the posting** (when the letter
is linked to an application), **Warmer** and **Shorter**. You can also open the Assistant with <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd> on Windows and Linux) to rework the letter; see [Using the Assistant](/guides/using-the-assistant).

<Frame caption="Web parity reference: review a paragraph against the linked posting before choosing Replace. Android and iPhone use the same canonical Improve service; this image is from the web editor.">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-improve-review.png"
    alt="Web letter Improve review showing one recorded request attempt through the fictional provider and fast model, original paragraph, suggested replacement and explicit Replace and Keep mine controls"
  />
</Frame>

On Android and iPhone, place the caret in a body paragraph and choose **Improve selected line** in the formatting toolbar. The review panel offers **Warmer** and **Shorter**, plus **Tie to the posting** when the letter has a linked application. It identifies the provider/model and the paragraph, surrounding letter text and linked posting that may be sent. Choose a preset, review the suggestion, then select **Replace** or **Keep mine**. A replacement is its own Undo step. A changed paragraph or a closed editor prevents a late replacement; recipient fields keep their formatting tools without AI rewriting.

On web and native mobile, the reviewed suggestion also shows its recorded request attempts and original model. **Replace** keeps those references alongside any earlier body-draft sources. Undo and Redo restore the corresponding wording and references together. Keeping your paragraph, saving, reopening or undoing does not generate another suggestion. Removing the provider does not erase saved request history; an older server may return a usable suggestion with unavailable history.

## Match your resume's design

By default, a letter linked to a resume uses the resume's template, type and colors, so the two always look like a pair. When you change the resume's design, the letter follows.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Design">
    Select **Design** in the editor bar, or press <kbd>2</kbd>.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep it matching, or give it its own design">
    With **Match** turned on, the letter follows the resume. Select **Change the resume's design** to open the resume in **Design**.

    <Frame caption="A letter can follow its resume’s design">
      <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-design-matching.webp" alt="Letter Design matching Product designer for Kiln Studio, with Template, Typography, Color, Page and a separate Language setting" />
    </Frame>

    Turn **Match** off to give the letter a design of its own. It keeps the current design as a starting point, and **Template**, **Typography**, **Color** and **Page** settings appear for the letter alone. Select a template to preview it, then **Use this template** to apply it.

    <Frame caption="Give a letter its own design">
      <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-design-own.webp" alt="Letter Design with matching turned off and its own template and design controls" />
    </Frame>

  </Step>
</Steps>

Turn **Match** back on at any time to follow the resume again. A letter with no resume chosen under **From** always has its own design.

When updating a letter through the API, setting `template` or `metadata` gives it its own design and turns matching off. Set `designLinked: true` in a later update to resume matching.

## Download the letter

Select **Download PDF** in the editor bar, or press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>P</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>P</kbd>). The PDF includes the header with your name and contact details, and is named after you, for example `David-Kowalski-Cover-Letter.pdf`.

For other formats, select the arrow beside **Download PDF**, or press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>E</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>E</kbd>). The download menu opens:

<Frame caption="Choose a letter download format">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-share-download.webp"
    alt="Letter download menu with file name, formats and Print"
  />
</Frame>

| Option              | What you get                                                                                                   |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **PDF**             | The letter exactly as it looks on the page. Best for applying.                                                 |
| **Resume + letter** | The linked resume and this letter as two PDFs, named to match. Shows only when the letter has a resume.        |
| **Word**            | A `.docx` file with simplified layout, including your name and contact details, for portals that ask for Word. |
| **OpenDocument**    | An editable `.odt` file with simplified layout, including your name and contact details.                       |
| **Markdown**        | Plain text with headings, to paste into application forms.                                                     |
| **JSON**            | A backup of the letter that imports back into Reactive Resume as a letter.                                     |

Review **File name**, then select a format to download it. **Print** is in this menu too.

In the native app, select **Download** to open **Download & history**. On a phone, the icon is labeled **Download & history**.

Letters have no public link. To share one, download it and attach the file.

When you download a resume from its download menu and the resume's application has a letter, you can also tick **Also download the … cover letter** (the label names the company) to get both files at once. See [Downloading your resume](/guides/exporting-your-resume).

## Letters and applications

For a saved job, **Use an existing letter** in application details links a letter you already wrote. The **Save a job** and **Edit details** forms also offer a letter picker. In Documents, resume and letter menus offer **Link to application…**; resume menus additionally offer **Write a letter…** with that resume selected. Builders link back to their application.

<Frame caption="A submitted application keeps its recorded letter. The picker explains why another letter cannot replace it.">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/submitted-letter-picker.png"
    alt="The letter editor's application picker with Lumen Labs disabled and labeled Letter already sent"
  />
</Frame>

- Each application has one letter. If you link a letter to an application that already has one, the new letter takes its place.
- When the application moves to **Applied** or a later stage, the letter as it reads then is saved in its **History** as the version you sent. The application shows it under **What you sent** as "Version sent" with the date.
- In **Documents**, a letter's menu has **Link to application…** to link or unlink it without opening it.

To see earlier versions of a letter, open **History** in the editor bar (the clock icon) which replaces the current panel. It works like resume history; see [Undoing changes and version history](/guides/undoing-changes-and-version-history).

On web and native mobile, restoring a named version keeps its name in the notice and the **Restored “…”** and **Before restoring “…”** entries. Unnamed versions keep the general restore labels.

## Manage a letter

Use **Undo** and **Redo** in the editor bar for letter-wide changes. In a text field, the keyboard shortcuts undo and redo your typing.

Select the letter's name in the editor bar to **Rename…**, **Duplicate**, **Lock editing** or **Move to Trash**. A locked letter can't be edited until you select **Unlock editing**. A letter in Trash can be restored for 30 days; see [Using the trash](/guides/using-the-trash).

<Note>
  In earlier versions, a cover letter could be a section inside a resume. Those letters are now letters of their own in
  **Documents**, named after the resume and the letter (for example "Frontend Resume — Cover Letter"), still linked to
  the resume's details and design. The resume no longer contains them. These older letters keep their recipient block as
  you wrote it under **To**, instead of separate name, company and date fields.
</Note>

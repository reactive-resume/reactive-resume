---
title: "Writing a cover letter"
description: "Create a cover letter as its own document, link it to a resume and a job application, match your resume's design, and download it."
---

In Reactive Resume, a cover letter is a document of its own. It sits next to your resumes in **Documents**, under the **Letters** tab. A letter can take your name, contact details and design from one of your resumes, and its recipient from a job application, so you only write the body.

## Create a letter

You can start a letter from an application or from **Documents**.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="From an application">
    Starting from the job you're applying for is quickest, because the letter is set up for that job.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the application">
        In **Applications**, select the application to open its details.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Select Write a letter">
        Under **What you sent**, select **Write a letter**. The button shows only while the application has no letter.

        <Frame caption="Review next steps and sent documents in the detail sheet">
          <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/application-write-a-letter.webp" alt="Fictional Northwind Games application detail sheet with stages, next technical interview, sent resume, Write a letter, salary, contact, notes and activity" />
        </Frame>
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    The new letter is named after the company, for example "Cover letter — Northwind Games". It becomes this application's letter, takes its recipient from the application (the company, and the first contact's name if there is one), and takes your details and design from the application's resume, if it has one.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="From Documents">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open New">
        In **Documents**, select **New**, or press <kbd>N</kbd>.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Select New cover letter instead">
        At the bottom of the dialog, select **New cover letter instead**.

        <Frame caption="One New dialog for resumes, letters and saved jobs">
          <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/new-dialog-cover-letter.webp" alt="New dialog offering Import a resume, Copy a resume for a job, Start blank, New cover letter instead, Save a job instead and Try with a sample resume" />
        </Frame>
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    Before starting the new letter, choose **Resume for a new letter**. Choose **No resume** to start independently. The new letter is named "Untitled letter". You can link an application and change the resume in the editor.

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

To bring back a letter you downloaded as JSON from Reactive Resume, import the file from the **New** dialog as you would a resume; it comes back as a letter. See [Importing resumes](/guides/importing-resumes).

## Fill in the letter

The letter editor looks like the resume editor, with two modes: **Write** and **Design**. The panel on the left holds the letter's details, and the page on the right shows the letter as it will print, updating as you type.

<Frame caption="Write a letter beside its rendered page">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-editor-write.webp"
    alt="Letter editor for fictional Priya Shah at Northwind Games, with sender details linked to Game Developer Resume and a rendered letter"
  />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="For: the application">
    Under **For**, select **Link an application** to choose the job the letter is for, or **Change** to pick another. Closed applications aren't listed, except the one this letter is already on. Applications with a different letter already recorded as sent are disabled and labelled **Letter already sent**. A letter recorded as sent keeps its application link. Linking fills the recipient's company and name. Choose **No application** to unlink it. Without an application, you fill in the recipient yourself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="To: the recipient">
    Under **To**, check **Name or team**, **Company** and **Date**. The greeting follows the name: "Dear Priya," for Priya Shah, or a greeting to the hiring team when the name is empty.
  </Step>
  <Step title="From: your details">
    Under **From**, choose the **Resume** your details come from. With **Use details from** that resume turned on, your name, headline, email, phone and location stay linked: when you change them on the resume, the letter changes too, and tells you the next time you open it.

    Turn the switch off to keep a copy of your details in the letter instead. Below the switch, edit everything the letter's header prints: **Full name**, **Headline**, **Email**, **Phone**, **Location**, **Website** and the extra contact fields and profile links under **Add field**. Clear a field, or remove an extra field, to leave it off the letter. If the resume has a photo, turn off **Show photo** to leave it off this letter; to change the photo itself, edit the resume. These edits change only this letter; your source resume stays unchanged. The copy doesn't change when the resume does.

    Choose **No resume** to write a standalone letter with the same sender fields. Removing a resume keeps the details and design currently shown. Sender edits update the preview, automatic signature and downloads, and save with the letter.

    Turning **Use details from** back on replaces the letter's independent details with the resume's current details. Choosing a resume for a standalone letter also replaces its sender details and links its design. Changing resumes while the sender switch is off keeps your independent details.

    <Frame caption="Link the letter sender to a resume">
      <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-from-resume.webp" alt="Letter sender linked to Game Developer Resume, with Use details from that resume enabled" />
    </Frame>

    <Frame caption="With No resume selected, sender fields belong to this letter. The preview and automatic signature use Jordan's saved details.">
      <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-sender-details.png" alt="Standalone letter with No resume selected, editable headline, website and profile links, fictional Jordan Reyes sender fields and the current Export action" />
    </Frame>

  </Step>
  <Step title="Letter: the body">
    Under **Letter**, select **Write it myself** and type the body. The greeting and sign-off are added around it in the letter's language. A letter created from an application starts in the posting's declared language, so the body, greeting, sign-off and date stay in one language. **Design → Language** changes the letter's language at any time, even while its design still mimics the resume: the design link shares template, type and colors, not the language, and your authored greeting, sign-off and words are never rewritten.

<Frame caption="Language is its own Design setting. This letter was created from a German posting, so its greeting, date and sign-off are German while its template, type and colors still match the resume.">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-design-language.png"
    alt="Letter Design panel with Match the resume on, a Language field set to German, and a German letter page with Einstellungsteam, 31. Oktober 2026 and Liebes Einstellungsteam"
  />
</Frame>

    Edit **Greeting** or **Sign-off** to use your own wording. **Sign-off** includes your name: edit the whole signature and add your phone, email or other details on lines below it. Custom signatures print exactly as entered, without an extra name appended. Clear either field to omit it, useful when you already wrote a closing in the body. Select **Use default** to restore the automatic wording and sender name.

    The body editor has the same formatting tools as the resume; see [Formatting text](/guides/formatting-text).

  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="Set a greeting and sign-off in your own words">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-greeting-sign-off.png"
    alt="Letter editor with Hallo Dana greeting and a German sign-off entered for fictional David Kowalski"
  />
</Frame>

Below the body, **Length** counts your words against the 180 to 320 words most recruiters read, and says whether the letter is short, comfortable or getting long. It's a guide, not a rule.

<Frame caption="Review the letter length">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-length-and-design.webp"
    alt="Letter length summary showing a short draft and guidance on brevity"
  />
</Frame>

Changes save automatically. The status under the letter's name in the editor bar shows **Saving…**, **Saved** or, if something went wrong, **Not saved** with **Retry**.

If saving stops with **Review saved version**, select **Reload**, then **Keep a copy and reload**. Your unsaved edits are saved as a separate letter in **Documents**, with “(mine)” added to its name, before the saved version opens. The copy keeps the text, sender details and design shown in your editor, without links to the resume or application. If copying or reloading fails, your edits stay open for retry. In the native app, **Keep both** offers the same copy-and-load choice.

## Draft the body with AI

If you've connected an AI provider, an empty body offers a first draft. See [Connecting an AI provider](/guides/using-ai) to set one up.

<Frame caption="Write the body yourself or choose optional AI drafting">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-body-empty.webp"
    alt="Empty letter draft with manual writing and optional drafting controls for the fictional linked application"
  />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start the draft">
    Select **Draft from the posting** when the letter is linked to an application, or **Draft from your resume** when it
    only has a resume. The draft uses only what the letter is linked to: the posting, your resume, or both, and it is
    written in the letter's language. The line beside the button names the provider it sends them to. Select **Stop** to
    cancel it while it's being written.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Refine it">
    When the draft is ready, ask for **Shorter** or **More personal** versions, or select **Discard** to drop it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep it">
    Select **Keep** to put the draft in the body. Until you do, the draft never replaces anything, and you can edit it
    freely afterwards. An unkept draft survives a reload in the same tab: it comes back with **Keep**, **Shorter**,
    **More personal** and **Discard**, so seeing it again doesn't cost another request. Leaving the editor with a draft
    streaming or waiting asks you to keep or discard it first.
  </Step>
</Steps>

To draft, a letter needs a linked resume or application. You can also open the Assistant with <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd> on Windows and Linux) to rework the letter; see [Using the Assistant](/guides/using-the-assistant).

## Match your resume's design

By default, a letter linked to a resume uses the resume's template, type and colors, so the two always look like a pair. When you change the resume's design, the letter follows.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Design">
    Select **Design** in the editor bar, or press <kbd>2</kbd>.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep it matching, or give it its own design">
    With **Match** turned on, the letter follows the resume. Select **Change the resume's design** to open the resume in **Design**.

    <Frame caption="Keep a letter matched to its resume design">
      <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-design-matching.webp" alt="Letter Design with Match Game Developer Resume enabled" />
    </Frame>

    Turn **Match** off to give the letter a design of its own. It keeps the current design as a starting point, and **Template**, **Type**, **Color** and **Page** settings appear for the letter alone. Hover over a template to preview it on the page.

    <Frame caption="Give a letter its own design">
      <img src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-design-own.webp" alt="Letter Design with matching turned off and its own template and design controls" />
    </Frame>

  </Step>
</Steps>

Turn **Match** back on at any time to follow the resume again. A letter with no resume chosen under **From** always has its own design.

When updating a letter through the API, setting `template` or `metadata` gives it its own design and turns matching off. Set `designLinked: true` in a later update to resume matching.

## Download the letter

Select **Download PDF** in the editor bar, or press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>P</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>P</kbd>). The PDF includes the header with your name and contact details, and is named after you, for example `David-Kowalski-Cover-Letter.pdf`.

For other formats, select **Export**, or press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>E</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>E</kbd>). The **Download & history** sheet opens on **Download**:

<Frame caption="Download a letter in the format you need">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/letter-share-download.webp"
    alt="Download & history for a letter showing available formats, paired resume download and editable file name"
  />
</Frame>

| Option              | What you get                                                                                                   |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **PDF**             | The letter exactly as it looks on the page. Best for applying.                                                 |
| **Resume + letter** | The linked resume and this letter as two PDFs, named to match. Shows only when the letter has a resume.        |
| **Word**            | A `.docx` file with simplified layout, including your name and contact details, for portals that ask for Word. |
| **OpenDocument**    | An editable `.odt` file with simplified layout, including your name and contact details.                       |
| **Markdown**        | Plain text with headings, to paste into application forms.                                                     |
| **JSON**            | A backup of the letter that imports back into Reactive Resume as a letter.                                     |

Edit **File name** if you like, then select the download button.

In the native app, select **Download** to open **Download & history**. On a phone, the icon is labelled **Download & history**.

Letters have no public link. To share one, download it and attach the file.

When you download a resume from its **Download & history** sheet and the resume's application has a letter, you can also tick **Also download the … cover letter** (the label names the company) to get both files at once. See [Exporting your resume](/guides/exporting-your-resume).

## Letters and applications

For a saved job, **Use an existing letter** in application details links a letter you already wrote. The **Save a job** and **Edit details** forms also offer a letter picker. In Documents, resume and letter menus offer **Link to application…**; resume menus additionally offer **Write a letter…** with that resume selected. Builders link back to their application.

<Frame caption="A submitted application keeps its recorded letter. The picker explains why another letter cannot replace it.">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/writing-a-cover-letter/submitted-letter-picker.png"
    alt="The letter editor's application picker with Lumen Labs disabled and labelled Letter already sent"
  />
</Frame>

- Each application has one letter. If you link a letter to an application that already has one, the new letter takes its place.
- When the application moves to **Applied** or a later stage, the letter as it reads then is saved in its **History** as the version you sent. The application shows it under **What you sent** as "Version sent" with the date.
- In **Documents**, a letter's menu has **Link to application…** to link or unlink it without opening it.

To see earlier versions of a letter, open **History** in the editor bar (the clock icon) or the **History** tab in **Download & history**. It works like resume history; see [Undoing changes and version history](/guides/undoing-changes-and-version-history).

## Manage a letter

Select the letter's name in the editor bar to **Rename…**, **Duplicate**, **Lock editing** or **Move to Trash**. A locked letter can't be edited until you select **Unlock editing**. A letter in Trash can be restored for 30 days; see [Using the trash](/guides/using-the-trash).

<Note>
  In earlier versions, a cover letter could be a section inside a resume. Those letters are now letters of their own in
  **Documents**, named after the resume and the letter (for example "Frontend Resume — Cover Letter"), still linked to
  the resume's details and design. The resume no longer contains them. These older letters keep their recipient block as
  you wrote it under **To**, instead of separate name, company and date fields.
</Note>

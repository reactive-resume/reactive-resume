# Reactive Resume v6 launch media research

Research date: 2026-10-02. Question: which media should accompany a founder's organic launch announcement on LinkedIn, X, and Show HN?

Recommended launch package: **single product image or short PDF document carousel on LinkedIn; product image on the first X post and native demo video as a follow-up; working app/demo link on Show HN, with optional media on the destination.** These are editorial recommendations informed by the evidence, not guaranteed reach rankings. Most observations are from 2025 or early 2026.

## LinkedIn: evidence and recommendation

**For a personal founder account, a strong product image or document carousel is the better supported reach default. Native video remains useful for demonstrating the product; there is no reliable evidence here that GIFs win reach.** This is a judgment from the studies below, not a guaranteed algorithm rule.

### Metricool: personal profiles, with actual impression data

Released April 14, 2026; 673,658 posts from 63,108 accounts worldwide, comparing January–February 2025 with January–February 2026. First edition including both personal profiles and company pages. [Metricool release and sample](https://metricool.com/press-release-linkedin-study-2026/).

The personal-profile table on printed page 28 gives these **averages per post**, visually checked against the PDF chart:

| Format            | Impressions | Interactions | Engagement rate |
| ----------------- | ----------: | -----------: | --------------: |
| Document carousel |    1,451.19 |        20.86 |           1.44% |
| Single image      |    1,186.74 |        21.51 |           1.81% |
| Text              |    1,045.15 |        12.20 |           1.06% |
| Poll              |      717.71 |         2.82 |           0.39% |
| Video             |      605.57 |        10.87 |           1.80% |
| Multi-image       |      338.42 |        12.56 |           3.71% |

Carousel/image impressions were approximately 2.40×/1.96× video. Multi-image's highest engagement rate coincided with the lowest impressions: engagement does not equal distribution. Impressions count displays, including repeats. Outliers stayed in; zero-impression/zero-interaction posts were excluded. Personal-profile engagement excludes clicks. Means do not isolate format from audience, content, or creator differences. The methodology does not clearly document exclusion of all promoted content. [Metricool study, pp. 28 and 41](https://metricool.com/wp-content/uploads/Linkedin-Study-2026-EN.pdf).

Personal analytics are restricted to posts published through Metricool, creating selection bias. [Metricool analytics scope](https://help.metricool.com/linkedin-personal-profile-metrics-h3usk).

### Buffer: engagement evidence, not a LinkedIn reach comparison

March 2026 report, 52M+ Buffer posts; latest data December 3, 2025. LinkedIn median engagement: carousel **21.77%**, video **7.35%**, image **6.52%**. No LinkedIn format reach table; video-for-reach is explicitly speculation. Profile/page split undisclosed; metrics differ from Metricool. [Buffer report](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/). Companion methodology inconsistently specifies followers as denominator. [Companion page](https://buffer.com/social-media-benchmarks/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026).

### Socialinsider: company pages only

Published March 16, 2026, with quarterly updates. Sample: **1.3M posts, 16,645 business pages, January 2024–December 2025**. Main “2026” benchmarks are actually 2025 observations; Q2 2026 engagement is a separate update.

2025 average engagement by impressions: document **7.00%**, multi-image **6.45%**, video **6.00%**, image **5.30%**. Average impressions for pages with 1–5K followers: document **525**, multi-image **490**, video **355**, image **315**; 5–10K: **550 / 1,210 / 590 / 430** respectively. These support documents or images as plausible alternatives, but cannot establish founder-profile behavior. The prose says multi-image wins for all pages below 50K; its own 1–5K table instead puts documents first. Claimed 36% decline concerns video views, not every format's reach. [Socialinsider original benchmarks and methodology](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/linkedin).

### AuthoredUp: corroborating profile data, with weaker methodological disclosure

Updated September 2, 2026; **3M+ personal-profile posts, March 2025–February 2026**. Reported reach multipliers relative to each profile's median: document **1.39×**, image **1.20×**, video **0.86×**; engagement multipliers **1.30× / 1.33× / 0.93×**. Images lead below 5K followers; documents lead at 20K–50K and 50K+. This makes a single image especially reasonable for a smaller founder audience.

Caveats: no account count, uncertainty intervals, fully reproducible aggregation method, or clear reach-field definition. Some prose contradicts tables: polls have higher reach than documents; image engagement multiplier exceeds document. Use reported tables as corroboration, not its universal algorithm explanations. [AuthoredUp format analysis](https://authoredup.com/blog/best-performing-content-on-linkedin).

The **36,946-video** duration subset reports >3-minute videos at **1.21×** typical video reach, versus 30–60 seconds at **0.95×**. However, its video guide dates the sample March 2025–February 2026, while its format article dates it October 2025–March 2026. This is insufficient justification to lengthen a launch trailer. Length also covaries with topic and creator. [AuthoredUp video analysis](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-video-posts), [conflicting sample window](https://authoredup.com/blog/best-performing-content-on-linkedin).

### What official LinkedIn guidance establishes

LinkedIn reported **36% year-over-year growth in video watching** in February 2025. Platform-wide growth does not establish greater reach per launch post. [LinkedIn product announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/up-36-over-last-year-video-linkedin-booming-time-now-somasundaram-yqbzc/).

Its March 12, 2026 engineering article describes ranking using professional relevance and historical reading, skips, dwell, likes, comments, and shares. It does not publish a universal media-format multiplier. [LinkedIn feed engineering](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/engineering-the-next-generation-of-linkedins-feed).

GIF uploads are supported, but none of the studies above isolates GIF performance. Support is not evidence of superior reach. [LinkedIn supported media](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a564109).

### Practical LinkedIn choice for this launch

- **Main anniversary post:** use a sharp image showing the actual v6 editor and finished resume. If a carousel is easy to reuse, make a short PDF with a launch cover, editor, templates, and two or three substantial features. Keep the personal six-year story in the caption. Single image is the simplest defensible option; carousel helps readers explore multiple changes.
- **Follow-up demonstration:** upload the existing showreel natively, with a readable cover, captions/on-screen explanation, and useful product action immediately. A suggested 30–60-second cut is an editorial choice for this launch, not a proven LinkedIn optimum. Do not pad it to match a duration benchmark.
- **GIF:** use only if one brief loop explains a feature more clearly. No evidence warrants choosing it for a reach boost.
- **Measure:** impressions/reach, outbound visits, and new signups. Engagement and video views are supporting metrics. A sequential launch and follow-up cannot isolate format effects because timing and audience fatigue differ.

All quantitative format studies here are observational. They describe their own user samples and dates; none randomly assigns the same Reactive Resume post to video, GIF, and image.

## X: evidence and recommendation

Buffer's March 5, 2026 report gives median engagement rates of **3.56% text, 3.40% images, 2.96% video, 2.25% links**. Its primary windows cover 2025, with latest observations through December 3. These are Buffer users' posts and engagement rates, not format-specific reach rankings. The evidence supports images as an engagement default, without proving greater distribution. [Original Buffer report](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/).

Metricool's 2026 cross-platform study covers **1,123,528 X posts from 15,116 accounts** in 2025. Video average interactions rose from **81.15 to 110.01**. Its X section does not supply a comparable video/image/GIF impression table. Absolute interaction averages cannot settle the reach question or contradict a median engagement-rate comparison by themselves. [Metricool report, pp. 4 and 58–59](https://metricool.com/wp-content/uploads/Social-Media-Study-2026-EN.pdf).

An older official X Business article reports **55% more engagements for GIF posts versus posts without GIFs**, from an internal study of 3.7M users. It uses 2019 examples, lacks a study window and detailed methodology, and does not compare GIFs against images or videos or measure reach. Treat it as historical marketing evidence, not a 2026 format ranking. [Original X Business article](https://business.x.com/de/blog/6-ways-gifs-twitter-strategy).

Official X Help says native videos and GIFs both autoplay, subject to viewers' settings; autoplay is not a GIF-only advantage. [X Help](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-videos).

**Practical choice:** attach a readable screenshot or product launch image to the first post. Use a native MP4 demonstration in a follow-up or thread reply. A 15–30-second cut, showing a useful action immediately and understandable without sound, is an editorial suggestion, not a proven optimal duration. GIF is reasonable for one short repeating action; no current primary comparison found establishes it as the reach winner.

## Show HN: product access matters more than attachment format

The standard Show HN listing displays titles and links, without native image/video/GIF feed attachments. This is a direct observation of the current first-party interface, not an engagement study. A thumbnail on the destination does not create a thumbnail in this list. [Current Show HN listing](https://news.ycombinator.com/show).

Show HN's official guidance prioritizes something readers can actually try, preferably without signup/email barriers, and rejects landing-page-only submissions. Major overhauls can qualify; small version updates generally do not. [Show HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html).

**Practical choice:** submit the working app or accessible demo. Show a clear screenshot and optional short demo video on that destination. Keep the real product easy to reach. No credible controlled media-format reach comparison found for Show HN.

If the submission links directly to a video, HN asks for **[video]** in the title. Current general guidelines also prohibit generated or AI-edited submissions/comments, so the previous AI-written HN draft should be treated as planning notes; the author should write the final HN text themselves. [HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html).

## Creative and measurement choices for this launch

- Make the static asset useful by itself: readable v6 editor and finished resume, product name/version, and a short free/open-source message. These are creative recommendations, not measured guarantees.
- For LinkedIn, distinguish a PDF document carousel from a post containing several separate images; the studies report materially different impression results.
- If using video, upload the file natively on LinkedIn/X, add a cover and captions/on-screen text, and start with the product's useful behavior. Do not require watching a long intro before seeing the app. LinkedIn supports custom thumbnails and captions. [LinkedIn video help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7174587).
- Compare post impressions, visits to the app, and useful downstream actions such as starting/exporting a resume. Do not treat autoplay video views as equivalent to visitors or unique people reached.
- An image announcement followed by a video demo is a distribution plan, not an A/B experiment: content, time, exposure, and audience differ.
